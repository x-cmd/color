# Memphis versus Neon

---

Two loud families, and they are often mistaken for each other because both look like "bright
colours on a dark-ish background" at a glance in a mood board. They are not the same thing, and
choosing between them is mostly a question of one variable: **what is the ground?**

---

## 1. The short version

Memphis is loud on **white**. Neon is loud on **black**.

Memphis is daylight-bright colour that reads as pigment — paint, ink, printed laminate. Neon is
fluorescent colour that reads as a **light source**: it glows because everything around it is
dark, and it does not survive being flattened into a white page.

Second difference, and it is nearly as important: **memphis uses many saturated colours at once and
lets them fight; neon keeps the accents few and lets the dark field do the work.** Memphis has six
chromatic colours and no dominant one. Neon has five accents and a near-black canvas that carries
most of the composition.

If you only take one line away: memphis is a daytime style and neon is a night-time style, and
they are not interchangeable between those conditions.

---

## 2. Side by side

| | Memphis | Neon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Surface | White `#FFFFFF` | Void `#05060A` |
| Second neutral | Black `#1A1A1A` as ink | Panel `#131722`, Text `#E6EDF3` |
| Chromatic members | 6 | 5 |
| Chroma band | `C` 0.12 – 0.25 | `C` 0.02 – 0.29 |
| Lightness band | `L` 0.55 – 0.91 | `L` 0.21 – 0.87 |
| Mean lightness | 0.72 | 0.68 |
| Mean chroma | 0.19 | 0.18 |
| Text on accents | Black works on all six | Light text works on all five, but only on the dark ground |
| Reads as | pigment, daylight, printed | light source, night, electric |
| Chrome count | many equal voices | few accents, one dark field |
| Failure mode | reads unserious | unreadable, tiring |

The two bands are numerically close — mean chroma 0.19 against 0.18, mean lightness 0.72 against
0.68 — and this is exactly why the families get confused. The colour *intensities* are almost the
same. What differs is the substrate: what the colour sits on, and how much room each colour is
given.

Look at the lightness band. Memphis occupies `L` 0.55–0.91: the narrowest spread of any family
here, with nothing dark except the ink. Neon occupies `L` 0.21–0.87, and that lower half — void at
0.12, panel at 0.21 — is where all of neon's character comes from. Memphis has no low end. Neon is
made of its low end.

---

## 3. Where they really differ

### 3.1 The ground decides the light

A memphis colour on white reads as paint. A neon colour on white reads as a mistake: neon cyan
`#00F0FF` against a white page has a contrast ratio of 1.41, which means it disappears. The same
colour on neon's void `#05060A` sits at 20.25 — the entire point of the family.

This is why the ground is not a design decision but a precondition. Memphis needs `#FFFFFF` to work
at all; neon needs `#05060A`. You cannot swap the substrates without swapping the families, and
you cannot gradually move one toward the other — a memphis palette on a dark grey page is neither
one nor the other, it is just a palette in the wrong light.

### 3.2 Pigment versus light source

Memphis colours are *material*. They are the colour of the object, the laminate, the printed
sheet. They have no glow, and the design does not pretend otherwise.

Neon colours are *emitted*. They read as tubes, screens, signs in the dark. The style leans on the
metaphor hard — glow effects, chromatic edges, a sense of something lit from behind — because the
flat colour alone cannot carry that idea on a dark field.

The practical test: ask whether the colour would still make sense if the lighting were explained.
A yellow square on a white poster is fine in daylight. A yellow square on a black page is fine
because it is *a sign in the dark*. Take a memphis layout and put it on black, and the colours
suddenly read as fluorescent — you have accidentally built a neon poster with the wrong palette.

### 3.3 Many colours versus few

Memphis runs six chromatic colours at full chroma with no dominant one. That is its whole
grammar: equal voices, deliberate friction, geometric shapes that misalign. The page is busy and
the busy-ness is the point.

Neon inverts this. Its canvas is nearly empty — void at `L` 0.12 and panel at 0.21 take up most of
the area — and the accents are sparse points of light. The composition is carried by the **dark
field**, not by the colour. Neon magenta `#FF2E88` on void is not one colour among many; it is a
single lit thing in a large darkness, and its drama depends on how much room around it there is.

So: memphis multiplies colour, neon rations it. That is the same difference that separates memphis
from macaron, in a different key — macaron rations colour too, but on a light ground, and quietly.
Memphis rations nothing.

### 3.4 What they share

They are not opposites in the way macaron and morandi are opposites. Both are loud, both are
high-chroma, and both refuse the middle of the road. What they disagree about is everything else:
the ground, the count, the metaphor, the hour of the day.

---

## 4. Choosing between them

Ask three questions in order.

**Is the product used in daylight, in ordinary light, on a screen someone stares at all day?**
Then memphis. Its colours were built for a white page in an office.

**Is the product used at night, in a dark room, by people who want to be impressed?**
Then neon. Developer tools, gaming, nightlife, a landing page for a launch at 11pm.

**Is it printed?** Memphis prints. Neon does not — a neon palette in CMYK turns into flat bright
squares with no glow and no relationship to the black it was designed against. The white paper
would have to be the black.

Then check the audience's expectation of seriousness. Both families are loud. Memphis is loud and
*playful* — ironic, retro, a bit cheeky. Neon is loud and *technical* — electric, synthetic,
forward-looking. A bank that wants to look modern should not reach for either, and a bank that
reaches for neon will look like a crypto startup.

---

## 5. Where people confuse them

- **"Both are high-chroma, so they're the same."** The chroma is similar; the structure is not. One
  family is a palette of many colours on white, the other is a set of accents on black. Look at
  the lightness band, not the chroma.
- **"Memphis is the older, more tasteful neon."** They come from different decades and different
  intentions. Memphis came out of Milanese design arguing against corporate rationalism; neon came
  out of night-time sign-making, film and electronic music. Memphis is a joke with a designer behind
  it; neon is an atmosphere.
- **"Just put the memphis colours on a dark background for a more modern look."** That does not
  produce memphis. It produces a dark page where the colours have lost their daylight quality and
  gained a glow they were never designed for — neon with the wrong accents.
- **"Neon is memphis but darker."** Neon's dark end is not memphis's absence of dark. Memphis's
  black `#1A1A1A` is ink on paper; neon's void `#05060A` is the absence of paper. One is a drawn
  line, the other is the room the line is in.

---

## 6. Using both

They can be combined, but only in one arrangement: **memphis as the surface, neon as the light.**
A memphis white page with neon-void blocks for emphasis, or a memphis illustration on a neon-dark
panel, keeps the family boundaries intact — the daylight material carries the structure, the
emissive colour is the point of attention.

What does not work is merging the palettes into a single list of twelve hex codes and calling it
a system. The two grounds cannot coexist on the same surface — a page is either white or it is
near-black. Pick one ground per surface, and let the other family appear only as an accent
block.

The third family in the neighbourhood is worth naming here, because it is where the confusion
between "quiet" and "loud" gets sorted out. Wabi-sabi is the opposite end of the same axis:
its band runs `C` 0.03–0.05 against memphis's 0.12–0.25, its mean lightness is 0.61 against
memphis's 0.72, and it is built on warm earth tones rather than primaries. The comparison is in
[wabi-sabi versus memphis](../../6-wabi-sabi/03-wabi-sabi-vs-memphis/en.md); the neon side is in
[neon origin](../../8-neon/01-neon-origin/en.md) and
[the neon palette](../../8-neon/02-neon-palette/en.md).

That gives a clean three-point scale: wabi-sabi quiet and earthy, memphis loud and daylight,
neon loud and nocturnal.
