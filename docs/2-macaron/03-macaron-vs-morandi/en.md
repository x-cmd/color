# Macaron versus morandi

---

A swatch labelled "morandi grey" sits on the same board as a swatch labelled "macaron pink",
they are within a few points of each other on lightness, and they are doing completely different
jobs. The difference is visible in three numbers and in one habit: how each family uses colour.

---

## 1. The short version

Macaron is a **bright, sweet, decorative** palette. Morandi is a **quiet, greyed, structural** one.

Macaron keeps seven hues legible and spends them on decoration. Morandi pulls four hues toward grey
and spends its colour on material — the surfaces and rules that build the page. Macaron's accent is a
coral that calls attention; Morandi's a deep umber that holds structure down. If the brief says
"cute", "friendly", "for a consumer app" — macaron; "quiet", "editorial", "considered" — morandi.

**Chroma.** Macaron's mean chroma is 0.07; morandi's is 0.03 — roughly half as chromatic. Macaron's
cream paper is neutral, at chroma 0.00; morandi's paper `#F5F2ED` sits at chroma 0.01 and hue 81° —
a warm grey, not a white.

**Lightness range.** Macaron's band spans sixteen points and morandi's spans thirty-one. Macaron
has room for one dark value — the ink — and no more. Morandi has room for a ladder: umber at
`L` 0.59, stone at `L` 0.61, taupe at `L` 0.77, sage at `L` 0.74, oat at `L` 0.90, paper at `L` 0.96.
Morandi builds structure from its own colours; macaron cannot, and trying breaks it.

## 3. Where they really differ

### 3.1 Greyed versus legible

Morandi's palette is not pale colours but colours pulled toward grey, per colour. Sage `#A3B18A` is
a green at `C` 0.06, hue 124°; clay `#DAD7CD` a warm grey at `C` 0.01; mauve `#C8B6C6` a violet at
`C` 0.03; mist-blue `#B0B7C6` a blue at `C` 0.02.

Macaron is the opposite. Each of its seven bases holds a readable amount of chroma — 0.06 to 0.07 —
and each announces itself: mint is green, lemon is yellow, lavender is violet. Ask someone to name
the colours in a macaron arrangement and they name them; ask about morandi and they say "beige" or
"grey" or "brown". Macaron's hues carry meaning without a label; morandi's carry only texture.

### 3.2 Decoration versus material

Macaron spends colour on what could have been plain: a card gets a pink wash and a mint border, an
illustration is drawn in lavender, a chip is filled with peach. Text, numbers and structure are
rendered in ink on cream.

Morandi spends colour on the material: the page is warm grey, the panels slightly different warm
greys, the rules are taupe, the accent an earth brown. A morandi layout in greyscale loses almost
nothing, because what it communicated through colour was texture and depth. Macaron in greyscale
loses its pastel character but keeps its hierarchy: coral reads as the one saturated thing.

### 3.3 The accent does opposite jobs

Macaron's accent is coral `#FFAAA5`, at `L` 0.82 and `C` 0.10 — the most chromatic colour in the
family, and the only one allowed to mean "act". Its job is to catch the eye: a coral button at `C`
0.10 against a page of `C` 0.07 colours is visible as the loudest thing in a quiet family, and
visible *alone*.

Morandi's accent is umber `#8C7A6E`, at `L` 0.59 and `C` 0.03 — the darkest structural colour in the
family and the one colour allowed to be genuinely dark. Its job is to anchor. On morandi's paper
`#F5F2ED`, umber scores 3.67; on morandi's oat `#E3DDD3`, 3.03. Those are not reading-contrast numbers
for body text — they are the numbers of a line, a rule, a mark, a button whose label sits on it in
ink. Macaron's accent is a highlight; morandi's a foundation.

### 3.4 The ink is not the same ink

Macaron's ink is a plum `#4A4045` — hue 346°, a red-leaning dark. Morandi's is `#3F3A38` — hue 43°,
a warm brown-black. Macaron keeps even its text cool and rosy; morandi's text is brown. Morandi's ink
is also stronger against its own surface: 11.05 against macaron's cream paper, against 9.81 for
macaron's ink against its own. Both are readable; the difference is temperature, not legibility.

## 4. Choosing between them

**Is the product asking to be liked, or to be trusted?**

Macaron is for products where emotion is the point — a food ordering app, a birthday card service, a
seasonal campaign. Morandi is for products where credibility is the point — a portfolio, a gallery,
a long-form editorial site, a lifestyle brand.

**Does the layout need a dark structural block?**

If yes, morandi. There is no version of "macaron with a dark header" that works — introduce a
near-black band and the page reads as decoration from another system. Morandi's umber at `L` 0.59 is
exactly the value macaron forbids, and morandi has a use for it.

**Does the interface need exactly one obvious action?**

Macaron is built for this: one coral action per screen, and the pastels around it are quiet enough
that the coral wins by default. Morandi's accent is deliberately unassertive; the primary action is
held by weight, size and position, not colour. If conversion depends on a button being unmissable,
macaron is the better foundation — if the brand can afford sweetness.

**Will the content be read carefully, for a long time?**

Morandi wins. Its greyer palette produces less optical noise across a long page, and its wider
lightness range lets a designer build hierarchy with value, not hue. Macaron on a long-form reading
page is charming on day one and tiring by day three.

And the honest reason people get it wrong: **a morandi palette is much harder to get right than it
looks.** Because its colours are so close to neutral, almost any pairing looks plausible and nothing
reads as an obvious error, so it takes real work to make morandi look intentional rather than washed
out. Macaron is easier to make cheerful and harder to make sophisticated, and that asymmetry matters
if you are choosing on effort.

---

## 5. Where people confuse them

**The "muted pastel" misreading.** Much of what gets called morandi in practice is macaron with the
saturation turned down in one colour rather than across a system. If the palette is still at `C` 0.07
and one card is grey, that is a macaron page with one dead element, and it looks like an accident.

**The "morandi is just beige" misreading.** Morandi has four hues, not one: sage, clay, mist-blue and
taupe have different hue angles (124°, 93°, 266°, 56°) and different chroma, so a page of nothing but
warm greys is a flatter thing. The mist-blue at hue 266° gives morandi its cool relief.

**The "macaron is for children" misreading.** Macaron's register is sweet, not juvenile. It works
well for adults who like dessert — a boutique hotel app, a florist, a craft-supply store, a wedding
planner. What makes it read as childish is not the palette but the *quantity*: large fields of
several pastels, plus rounded corners, plus a friendly face.

**The "they're both pastels, so mix them" misreading.** See below.

## 6. Using both

You can put them in the same product, but only if they stay in separate rooms.

**The workable split is structural.** Morandi for the chrome — the navigation, the footer, the
sidebar, the page background, the cards — and macaron for the moments of emphasis: a promotional
tile, an onboarding illustration, an empty-state graphic, a seasonal banner, a chip set. Morandi's
warm greys sit under macaron's pastels without competing, because morandi has no chroma to compete
with. The reverse is harder: macaron chrome with morandi accents reads as two teams disagreeing.

**The palette numbers hold across the boundary.** Morandi's ink `#3F3A38` against macaron's bases
runs from 5.71 (lavender) to 9.82 (lemon) — readable. Macaron's ink `#4A4045` against morandi's
colours runs from 8.91 (paper) down to 4.37 (sage), and 4.74 against taupe — clear of 4.5, but check
the bottom of that range. So text works in either direction; only the *accent* cannot be shared:
coral and umber cannot be two equal buttons on one screen — coral reads as the loud one, umber as a
rule. Hence one accent per screen. If a screen needs the macaron accent, the morandi accent belongs
only in structure: a border, a divider, a mark.

**Do not blend the two palettes into one swatch set.** A "macaron morandi" hybrid — desaturated
pastels — is the worst of both: Morandi's greys are warm and specific, macaron's clean and
legible.
